SOURCE F: Explaining a Japanese victory, not only an Allied defeat

Type: Prepared summary of a historian's argument, not a Japanese wartime document.
Prepared: 28 October 2026.
Based on: Brian P. Farrell, The Other Side of the Hill: Western Interpretations of the Japanese Experience in the Malayan Campaign and Conquest of Singapore, 1941–42, NIDS Military History Studies Annual, no. 19, 2016, pp. 80–96; especially pp. 80–85.
Reference: https://www.nids.mod.go.jp/publication/senshi/pdf/201603/09.pdf

Farrell argues that explaining a campaign requires investigating both armies. An account that examines Allied decisions carefully but treats Japanese forces as an anonymous enemy offers an incomplete explanation.

He also identifies difficulties with the evidence: records were destroyed, access to Japanese-language material was uneven, and later memoirs influenced interpretations. Historians must compare accounts rather than assume that an official or participant account is neutral.

Source limitation: This is an argument about how historians explain the campaign. It is useful for evaluating explanations, but is not direct evidence of what Yamashita knew or ordered on a particular day. The wording above is a modern paraphrase.
