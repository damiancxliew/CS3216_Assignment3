On 12 February: "breaks in the mains from bombing and shelling began to gain steadily over repairs".

On 14 February the Municipal Water Engineer, Mr Murnane, reported "that, though both pumping stations were still working, well over half the water was being lost; that all civil labour had disappeared". He estimated "that the water supply would last for 48 hours at the outside and that it might only last for 24 hours."

Percival described the town that day:

"The only newspaper being published was a Government controlled single sheet newspaper of which free issues were made. Practically all offices, business houses and shops were closed."

"The Peirce and MacRitchie water reservoirs were in enemy hands although water, whether by design or oversight, continued to flow to the pumping stations."

He put the civil population at "nearly one million". The Australian War Memorial talk describes "more than a million civilian residents and refugees".

Source limitation: Percival was explaining, four years later, why he surrendered; the water crisis was central to that case. The Japanese could have cut the reservoirs and did not, which this source cannot explain. Wording checked against a volunteer transcription, not the Gazette page.
