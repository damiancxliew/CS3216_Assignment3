The Volunteers. Among those who died in the first air raid was Corporal Raymond Lee Kim Teck of the Straits Settlements Volunteer Force.

Dalforce. Dalforce was made up of some 4,000 Chinese volunteers "from all walks of life". Percival wrote that "Owing to lack of weapons it could only be partially armed." Jack Ng Kim Boon, who lived near its headquarters, recalled: "they are brave but their training is far too short for them to know anything". Madam Cheng Seang Ho and her husband joined in January 1942, though both were in their 60s.

The Malay Regiment. Some 1,400 men in two battalions helped defend Pasir Panjang. On 13 February, C Company held Pasir Panjang village "despite being heavily outnumbered", then moved to Bukit Chandu, where it fought on 14 February. Second Lieutenant Adnan Saidi had chosen a motto for his platoon: "Biar putih tulang, jangan putih mata", which the article explains as "death before dishonour". The regiment lost 159 lives at Pasir Panjang. Percival wrote that the Malay Regiment "especially distinguished themselves".

Source limitation: These are later commemorative accounts; they honour the dead and are not neutral battle analyses. Jack Ng spoke decades later. The same article names Lee's unit as the Straits Settlements Volunteer Force in its text and the Singapore Volunteer Corps in a caption. This summary deliberately leaves out the descriptions of killing in the original article.
