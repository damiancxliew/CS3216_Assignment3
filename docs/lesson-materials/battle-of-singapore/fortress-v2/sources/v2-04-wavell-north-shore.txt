SOURCE V2-04: "little or nothing was done": the undefended north shore

Type: (a) Verbatim primary extracts from a wartime telegram, and (b) Churchill's later account, both as quoted in a 2015 article.
Prepared: 11 November 2026.
Based on: Fred Glueckstein, Churchill and the Fall of Singapore, Finest Hour 169, International Churchill Society, 2015.
Reference URL: https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-169/churchill-and-the-fall-of-singapore/

On 15 January 1942 Churchill asked General Wavell, the Allied commander for the region, what would happen if the army were forced back into the island. Wavell replied on 16 January:

"Until quite recently all plans were based on repulsing seaborne attacks on island and holding land attack in Johore"

"little or nothing was done to construct defences on north side of the island to prevent crossing Johore Straits, though arrangements have been made to blow up the causeway."

In his war history, The Hinge of Fate, Churchill later listed the reasons he had been given for this, including:

"the fact that the Army’s role was to protect the naval base, situated on the north shore of the island, and that it was therefore their duty to fight in front of that shore and not along it."

He answered: "I do not consider these reasons valid. Defences should have been built."

He also took a share of the blame: "I ought to have known."

Source limitation: Wavell's words come from a telegram quoted in a later magazine article, not from the original. Churchill's lines were written after the war, in a history that also defended his own record; his stated surprise is his own later account. The article's editorial insertion after "Johore" is left out here. Neither man was on the island's north shore when he wrote.
