1. A government notice, September 1941. By September 1941 there were still no public air-raid shelters. A government notice said that Singapore's low, flat land, with water near the surface, made underground shelters impossible. It told people to "find out where the nearest open space is, and make for that in air raid". It also said that the "publication of this notice need cause no alarm", and:

"On the contrary, the position in the Far East at the moment is calmer than it has been for several months past"

The first bombs fell barely three months later.

2. An Order of the Day, 8 December 1941. Later that day, after the first bombing, the British commander-in-chief of the Far East, Air Chief Marshal Brooke-Popham, issued an Order of the Day which began "We are ready" and went on:

"Our preparations have been made and tested; our defences are strong and our weapons efficient."

Source limitation: Both texts were written to keep the public calm and the troops confident; they show what officials wanted people to believe, not how strong the defences were. The wording was checked on 11 November 2026 against the two articles that quote them, not against the originals. Finest Hour cites Colin Smith, Singapore Burning (2005), for the Order of the Day.
