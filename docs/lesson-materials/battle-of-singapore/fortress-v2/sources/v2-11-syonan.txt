Following the capitulation, Sergeant John Nevell wrote: "When it suddenly ceased, the silence seemed to hurt."

15 February 1942 was the first day of Chinese New Year. Singapore was renamed Syonan-to, "Light of the South".

On 20 February the first issue of The Shonan Times was printed at the former premises of The Straits Times. The Japanese-run papers promoted ideas such as "Asia for Asiatics" and carried little objective news.

Life became hard. Ismail bin Zain remembered rationed rice: "We chopped the tapioca into small pieces, combined it with the rice and used it as rice".

Some of the harm was far worse. A Roots.gov.sg essay records that screening of the Chinese community resulted in the killing of thousands of Chinese men, remembered as Sook Ching.

Source limitation: Nevell's words are quoted by a later Australian article, which does not date his writing. Ismail bin Zain was interviewed long afterwards. This file mentions Sook Ching only briefly; teachers should decide how to treat it with younger students.
