On strength, Percival estimated that the Japanese could put three divisions against the island, "a total of about 60,000 men". Of his own army he wrote: "I believe it to have been in the neighbourhood of 85,000", adding: "Probably about 70,000 of the total were armed and equipped. A large number of these were very inadequately trained."

On where the attack would come, he wrote:

"I expected the attack to develop from the west, combined perhaps with a sea-borne attack via the Straits of Malacca. I thought it probable that another force would come down the Johore River to attack either Tekong Island or Changi."

On how he divided his forces: the Northern Area, from Changi to the Causeway, was held by "3 Indian Corps, now consisting of 11 Indian and 18 British Divisions with Corps troops". The Western Area, west of the Causeway, was held by "The Australian Imperial Force and the 44 Indian Infantry Brigade, with attached troops."

Source limitation: Percival wrote this four years later, partly to answer critics; his foreword complains of writers with "but a cursory knowledge of Malayan conditions", and he says some documents were lost. His figures are estimates. The wording was checked against a volunteer transcription, not the Gazette page, which refused automated access. His statement that he expected the west conflicts with Source V2-06.
