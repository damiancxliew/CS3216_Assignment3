SOURCE V2-03: Sardines, shelters and the last ships

Type: (b) Later recollections. Short extracts from National Archives of Singapore oral-history interviews, as quoted in a 2023 essay, with prepared connecting text.
Prepared: 11 November 2026.
Based on: Mark Wong, In Their Own Voices: Preparing for War in Singapore, BiblioAsia (National Library Board), vol. 18 issue 4, Jan–Mar 2023.
Reference URL: https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/vol-18/issue-4/jan-mar-2023/preparing-war-singapore

In early 1941 the authorities encouraged people to stockpile food, then tried to discourage it when prices rose. Families bought tinned food anyway. Soh Guan Bee, who was 13 at the time, said:

"Till today I dare not eat sardine, I see sardine I get sad."

Tan Ban Cheng, also 13, remembered that "at least two to three weeks before the surrender, most of us could not get any food from outside."

Without a garden to dig in, Chu Shuen Choo, a teacher in Katong, recalled: "[M]y shelter was just underneath the staircase and a table".

Gay Wan Guay joined the Air Raid Precautions. His job was to tell people near the coast to "move away from the Johor Straits as the Japanese were advancing", but he found it hard to persuade them to leave their farms, pigs and poultry.

Rajabali Jumabhoy, president of the Indian Chamber of Commerce, arranged ships for Indian evacuees: "Very few could leave, say about 5,000 and no more." He added: "Mostly the well-to-do people left from here and also Malaysia."

Source limitation: These people were interviewed decades later, and the essay chose these extracts to show preparation; they are not a survey of everyone in Singapore. Jumabhoy speaks as a community leader who himself left on 7 February 1942. The quotations were checked against the essay, not the recordings.
