SOURCE V2-02: The first raid and the planes overhead

Type: (b) Later recollections (oral-history extracts published by the National Archives of Singapore), with (c) a prepared summary of a government heritage page.
Prepared: 11 November 2026.
Based on: National Archives of Singapore, WWII Comes to Singapore; SG101 (Singapore Government), #OnThisDay: First Japanese Air Raid in Singapore on 8 Dec 1941.
Reference URL: https://corporate.nas.gov.sg/discover-archives/media/ww2comestosingapore/
Reference URL: https://www.sg101.gov.sg/resources/archives/onthisday-first-air-raid-in-singapore/

Summary: On 8 December 1941, 17 Japanese bombers attacked the city and the airbases. The raid left 61 dead and 133 injured. Air-raid drills and blackout exercises had been held before the war, but on the night many people ran into the streets in panic, and some thought it was only an exercise. Some people who alerted the authorities were told the raid was only a practice. By January 1942 there were two to three air raids a day.

Arshak Catchatoor Galstaun (interviewed 1982, accession 000170/7) remembered what the raids did to people's confidence:

"we thought that the British were so mighty that nothing could… no one could touch them."

"to see these planes coming 27 or 9 at a time sailing through the sky, with not a bit of opposition."

Valuppillai s/o Pandarapillai, a civil-defence warden (interviewed 1983, accession 000339/4), recalled a later raid:

"We had to assist the people who were wounded, to carry them and take them to the first aid, medical aid depot"

Source limitation: The two hosts disagree on the time of the first raid: the National Archives says "shortly after 4am"; SG101 says "around 4.30am". The witnesses spoke forty years later; "27 or 9 at a time" is a memory, not a count, and Valuppillai himself was unsure of the date of the raid he described. The quotations were checked against the published selection, not the full recordings.
