# The Fortress That Fell, v2: "What Will You Carry?" (design outline)

Prepared 11 November 2026. Design only: nothing here is built. Sources: the old pack (A–H, `../0*.txt`) and the new pack (V2-01 to V2-11, `sources/`). Each quoted word is checked in `sources/EVIDENCE.md`. The design runs on the current spec (2.1.1) with no schema or engine changes.

**Driving question (first screen):** *Everyone says Singapore can never fall. What do you believe, and what will you do for the people who believe it too?*
**Role:** Lim Siew Lan ("Lan"), 15, a fictional volunteer runner for an Air Raid Precautions (ARP) post in Chinatown. Lan has no power over armies, only over what Lan carries, whom Lan tells, and what Lan keeps. The arc runs from belief (S1) to seeing (S2) to remembering (S3). Time: about 7 + 8 + 8 minutes, plus 3 minutes for the ending.
**Real people never speak.** Percival, Wavell, Yamashita, Tsuji and Churchill appear only in documents. Every speaking character is fictional, and each fictional character is labelled as fictional.
**The archive drawer.** Each stage has one clearly labelled drawer, "Remembered later". It holds recollections and later accounts. No character can read these, so a character in 1941 never knows anything later.

## Stage 1: "Sirens after the bombs" (Chinatown, 8–10 December 1941). The exhibition cut.
*Question:* The notice says there is no cause for alarm. Should your family believe it?
*Rooms (3):* the family shophouse (start; a newspaper on the counter), the ARP post in a school hall (stretchers, a siren, the notice), Raffles Place after the raid (a crowd, a news-sheet stall).
*Cast:* **Uncle Lim** (major, decider; a shopkeeper who trusts the notice). His stances run *Trusting* → *Uneasy* → *Ready to act*. Evidence that answers the notice moves him one stance. **Mrs Ong** (major, ARP volunteer, carried over from v1; stances *Steady* → *Shaken* → *Clear-eyed*). She defends the notice because it keeps her volunteers calm. The Order of the Day plus any contradicting note opens her up. **Ravi** (minor, 14, a newsboy). He knows that the sirens sounded late and that people who phoned in were told "it's a practice" (V2-02). Side quest: the scrap-iron and exercise-book drive (V2-03 host).
*Documents (3, each with an interpretive check):* (1) **Government notice, Sept 1941**: real words in a reconstructed layout. It is the slanted source (V2-01). (2) **Order of the Day, 8 Dec**, at the post: real words (V2-01). (3) **Drawer: "Remembered later"**: Galstaun and Mosbergen/Barth, the sardines and the shelter under the stairs (V2-02, V2-03, E).
*Events:* the 4 a.m. bombs (opening); news on 10 December that *Prince of Wales* and *Repulse* have been sunk (A); a siren if Lan lingers.
*Minimum path:* read the notice → talk to Mrs Ong → one more note (Ravi or the drawer) → Uncle Lim. *Alternatives:* Ravi alone can stand in for the drawer; the Order of the Day can stand in for Mrs Ong.
*Decision (Lan's own act; Uncle Lim decides):* (a) **Stay and keep the shop open**: the notice is right. (b) **Take Ah Ma to relatives in Yio Chu Kang.** This is *historical*: many families left the city (V2-03 host, Tan Guan Chuan). (c) **Stock tins and dig a shelter under the stairs.** (d) *Hidden:* **"Join the post, and bring the street with us."** It unlocks when Mrs Ong is *Clear-eyed*, Uncle Lim is *Ready to act*, and Lan has a drawer note. Every outcome ends with the same historical caption about two to three raids a day in January (V2-02).
*Carries forward:* where Ah Ma is, Mrs Ong's trust, and which opening narration S2 uses (situations).

## Stage 2: "The quiet shore" (north-west coast near Lim Chu Kang, 6–8 February 1942). Different in kind: night, the front line, uncertainty.
*Question:* Guns in the east, boats in the west. Where is the danger, and who will you get out?
*Basis:* ARP volunteers told people to "move away from the Johor Straits" (V2-03), and Percival ordered a half-mile belt cleared (despatch para 462).
*Rooms (4):* road junction with the ARP lorry (start), a kampong farm by the strait with a jetty and a sampan, an Australian section post in the mangroves, a Dalforce hut near the mouth of Sungei Kranji.
*Cast:* **Mdm Goh** (major, decider; a pig farmer). Her stances run *Staying* → *Wavering* → *Packing*: "My pigs are my whole life." **Private Tom Doyle** (minor; 22nd Australian Brigade, fictional). His slant is "we can hold this beach". Shown the clearance order, he admits that three battalions are covering the whole coast (V2-06). Side quest: he fought in Johor and talks about Gemas (B). **Ah Seng** (minor; a hawker who joined Dalforce, fictional): "brave but… training far too short", a shotgun (V2-08). **Mr Nair** (minor; ARP section leader, fictional) sends Lan out and wants Lan back by dark.
*Documents (3):* (1) **Clearance order**: a reconstruction based on despatch para 462 (V2-05 host). (2) **Drawer: "Two accounts of the plan"**: Percival's own words, "I expected the attack to develop from the west", set beside the Heritage Board's statement that he "believed… from the east" and the troop allocation (V2-05 vs V2-06). The check asks which account is more trustworthy and why. Both have motives. (3) **Drawer: "The other side, remembered"**: Yamashita's "bluff", Tsuji's bicycles, and Farrell's view that the bluff claim is "nonsense" (V2-07, F).
*Events:* 7 February: guns thunder in the east, towards Pulau Ubin (news). 8 February, night: a massive artillery barrage opens from across the strait (news; V2-06 host). A late summons from Mr Nair.
*Minimum path:* read the order → talk to Doyle or Ah Seng → Mdm Goh. *Alternatives:* either drawer; the soldier or the volunteer.
*Decision (Lan's own act, under uncertainty; Mdm Goh decides):* (a) **Follow orders: get Mdm Goh out now and go back to the city.** This is *historical*: the belt was cleared. (b) **Run to Mr Nair to report how thin the line is, and the guns in the east.** He passes it up. The army already knew; nothing changes. (c) **Stay and help her move the pigs.** The barrage catches them; they leave in the night with almost nothing. (d) *Hidden:* **"Leave now, take what we can carry."** It unlocks when Mdm Goh is *Packing* and Lan has notes from both Doyle and Ah Seng. Every outcome ends: "That night the Japanese landed between Lim Chu Kang and Sarimbun" (V2-06, C).
*Carries forward:* whether Mdm Goh is in the city in S3 (a situation placement) and Mr Nair's trust.

## Stage 3: "Forty-eight hours of water" (the city, 14–15 February 1942). A race, and two messages.
*Question:* The water is running out. Which message do you carry, and what will you keep when it is over?
*Rooms (4):* the Municipal Offices (City Hall), a street standpipe queue, the ground-floor ward in the Cathay Building, now a temporary military hospital (V2-09), and the ARP post (start).
*Cast:* **Mrs Ong** (decider; her stance is set by S1). **Mr Chia** (minor; a clerk to the Municipal Water Engineer, fictional). He holds the estimate: "48 hours at the outside" (V2-09). **Corporal Hamid** (minor; Malay Regiment, wounded, from v1). His story is Pasir Panjang and the motto "death before dishonour", with no graphic detail (V2-08, C). **Mdm Goh** or **Uncle Lim**, depending on earlier situations.
*Two messages (the soft choice):* the opening events hand Lan two notes, *the water estimate* and *the hospital is out of water*. Lan shows each note to someone (`notePassed`). Carrying the estimate to Mrs Ong first sets off "water parties formed"; carrying the hospital note first makes Hamid *open* and unlocks the hidden act. Either order works. A clock-driven news event marks the falling water pressure.
*Documents (3):* (1) **Percival's notes on the city, 14 February**: real words (V2-09). (2) **Telegrams of the last five days**: the letter dropped by air, Wavell's "fight on" and then "discretion", Percival's last telegram (V2-10, D, G). (3) **Drawer: "Afterwards"**: Nevell's silence, Syonan-to, the new newspaper, rice and tapioca, and one sober line on Sook Ching (V2-11), which the teacher can switch off.
*Decision (15 February, night; the order comes to disband the post):* (a) **Burn the logbook and the uniforms, as instructed.** This is *historical*: Ang Seah San's post did this (V2-03 host). (b) **Bury the logbook in a tin**, to dig up "after". (c) **Keep it**: "the city should remember". Mrs Ong warns that a photo or a list of names can bring the Japanese to a family's door (V2-03 host, Tan Wah Meng). (d) *Hidden:* **"Burn the names, keep the story."** It unlocks when Mrs Ong is *Clear-eyed*, Lan has carried the hospital note, and Lan has Hamid's note. Endings: *Ashes* (historical), *The tin*, *The record*, *The story without names* (secret). Every ending says the same thing: the surrender was signed at the Ford Factory on 15 February (D, V2-10).

**Interview (a museum guide at the Former Ford Factory, today):** "They called it an impregnable fortress. Why did it fall in a week?" There are three defensible verdicts. Each is counterpointed with notes that complicate it: **(1) The fortress was a belief more than a defence:** the notice, "little or nothing" built on the north shore, no fleet (V2-01, V2-04, A). Counterpoint: the Malay Regiment and the Australians fought hard. **(2) A bold enemy took a calculated risk:** speed, bicycles, the feint, and the gaps scouted in the north-west (V2-06, V2-07, B). Counterpoint: Yamashita's own words say he was weak. **(3) The city could not live without water:** a million people, broken mains, a 24-hour forecast (V2-09, V2-10). Counterpoint: the reservoirs were already lost, so why did the water still flow? The general counter is Farrell: study both armies.
**Reveal twist (what the defenders could not know):** the guns Lan heard in the east were meant "to further reinforce the bluff". Japanese scouts had already found the "lack of depth" in the north-west. Afterwards Yamashita said he was "very frightened" that the British would discover his weakness, and a historian calls that claim nonsense. Percival wrote in 1946 that he expected the west, yet two divisions waited in the Northern Area, east of the Causeway. *Everyone who was there told it in a way that suited them, and that is why you weigh sources.* Close with Churchill's "Singapore has fallen" (H).

## Art needs (how imported art works)
Stage images: `authoring.stageImages[stageId]` (in the zip), the media panel (upload, Commons, LoC or AI), or the generated `stage-<index>.webp` from `v3StagePaintingPrompt`. Portraits: key `charId:stanceId` for majors and `charId` for minors, set in `authoring.portraitImages`, by `gen-art.ts` (`stancePortraitRequests` + OpenAI, needs `OPENAI_API_KEY`), or by the app's portrait job after import. Sprites: by default, a regex on the name and title picks one of the 22 bundled fantasy sprites. The player is always "Boy" unless `walkingSprites.player` is custom, and a custom sprite is a 4×4 down/up/left/right sheet. Rooms have no images. The tiles, fixtures and weather come from keywords in the room descriptions and timePlace ("jetty", "boat" → harbour; "newspaper", "city hall" → civic; "night"). Need: **3 stage images**: S1 an AI painting of Raffles Place at dawn; S2 an AI painting of the Johor Strait at dusk; S3 reuse `art/stage-2-s2.jpg` (Asahi photo, public domain). **Portraits, all fictional, so AI:** Uncle Lim ×3, Mrs Ong ×3, Mdm Goh ×3, and one each for Ravi, Doyle, Ah Seng, Nair, Chia and Hamid: 15 in all. **Sprites:** bundled picks through `walkingSprites` (Woman for Mrs Ong and Mdm Goh, OldMan for Uncle Lim, Boy for Ravi; Knight or Hunter for Doyle, Hamid and Ah Seng are anachronistic). A girl player sprite needs a custom sheet, or the player becomes a boy.

## Top risks
1. A teenage runner is plausible but not documented, so it must be labelled clearly. 2. Every document must carry a reading check, so the quizzes must stay interpretive and the evidence held by people must grow. 3. "Remembered later" drawers inside 1941 scenes can blur chronology, so they need strong visual labels. 4. Sensitive material (Sook Ching, Alexandra Hospital, Adnan's death) stays out of play. 5. The despatch text comes from a volunteer transcription; check it against the Gazette scan by hand. 6. Marking one personal choice as "historical" can read as "the right answer", so the epilogues must say "many people did this", not "correct". 7. 15 portraits plus WWII-unfit fantasy sprites. 8. The LLM may give characters hindsight; the noLanding-style rules from v1 must carry over.

## Bolder alternative (5 lines)
*"Two shores":* alternate the stages between Lan in Singapore and a fictional Japanese conscript in Johor during the same week.
Both read the same night of 8 February from opposite sides of the strait, and both end on the road to the Ford Factory.
The Japanese stage would use only labelled later recollections (Tsuji, Yamashita) plus verified Japanese-language material, which this pack does not yet have.
The interview would ask: "Which side's story of the bluff do you believe?"
It needs 2–3 more verified Japanese sources (for example NIDS *Senshi Sōsho* extracts) before it is honest.
