SOURCE A: A naval base without the promised fleet

Type: Prepared secondary-source summary, not a wartime document or quotation.
Prepared: 28 October 2026, for a lower-secondary history generation trial.
Based on: Royal Australian Navy, The 'Singapore Strategy' and the fall of Singapore.
Reference: https://www.navy.gov.au/about-navy/history/history-milestones/singapore-strategy-fall-singapore

The plan for defending British interests in Asia depended on more than Singapore's naval base. Britain would send a battle fleet to the base if Japan threatened the region. Australian defence planning also relied heavily on this promise.

When Japan attacked in December 1941, the promised fleet had not arrived. A smaller force, Force Z, included the battleship Prince of Wales and battlecruiser Repulse. Japanese aircraft sank both ships off Malaya on 10 December.

Building a base and having a fleet available to use it were different things. The ships' destruction showed the danger posed by aircraft to naval forces.

Source limitation: This is a later Australian naval account. It explains the naval strategy's failure, but cannot by itself explain every land battle or the final surrender. The wording above is a modern paraphrase.
