## Description (260 char limit)

> Teachers drop in historical sources; we build a small world around them, with people
> who each want something different. Students investigate, decide under a timer, and get
> a debrief that shows what the record actually says — with the page and the quote — next
> to what the simulation assumed.

## First comment (the maker's note)

> Hi Product Hunt 👋
>
> We built this because "AI generates a history game" is the easy half, and the half that
> makes it unusable in a classroom is the other one: a generated world states things that
> never happened, in the same confident voice it uses for things that did.
>
> So the whole product is organised around one distinction. Every historical claim in the
> ending debrief carries the source, page and verbatim quote it was grounded in. Anything
> the record doesn't cover is shown separately and labelled as an assumption the
> simulation made. A student can check us, and disagreeing with the simulation is a valid
> outcome of the lesson.
>
> The rest of the design follows from teaching realities rather than demos: publishing is
> immutable, so editing an adventure mid-lesson makes a new version instead of changing
> the one 30 students are currently in; stage deadlines are held on the server, so a
> refresh doesn't buy more time; and a student who closes the tab resumes exactly where
> they left off, because the state was never in the tab.
>
> Free to try — bring a PDF you already teach from. We'd love to hear what breaks.
