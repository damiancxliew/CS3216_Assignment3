## Briefing (about 2 minutes)
> *You are Daniel Tan, a liaison officer in the Prime Minister's Department. You were seconded between Singapore
> and Kuala Lumpur after merger. Both sides trust you a little, and neither trusts you completely. If the
> federation fails, so does your career.*

The private brief adds: *you want the federation to survive; you fear being blamed by either side; you know the
April 1964 election results, but not what either leader intends next.* [G3–G3.5]

## Stage 1: After the elections (about 15 minutes)
**Central question,** always visible: *Can the PAP and UMNO still work together after the elections?* [P7]

1. **Exploring.** Daniel walks to the Residency using "go to a room" [P15.1]. Nothing shows him where the Tunku
   is; he has to find him [P15.2, P15.3].
2. **A document.** On a desk lies the *Utusan Melayu* editorial, with masthead and date and a small archive stamp
   showing it's a real excerpt [P4, P4.1, G10.1]. Reading it creates no note [P13.1]. Daniel selects the line
   calling the PAP's campaign a threat to Malay rights. It overlaps a key passage, so a note appears [P13.2]:
   > 📝 *The Utusan editorial calls the PAP a threat to Malay rights.*
3. **The Tunku is guarded.** His portrait looks polite but closed [P6.3]. Daniel asks about the elections. The
   Tunku's reply conveys a surface discovery, so a note appears [P7.1, P7.2]:
   > 📝 *The Tunku feels the PAP went back on its word over the mainland seats.*
4. **An unearned claim.** Daniel types: *"Lee told me he regrets contesting those seats."* He has no such note.
   The Tunku answers: *"Did he? Then why do I hear it from you and not from him?"* Nothing changes in the game
   [P8.2, P8.3].
5. **Stuck.** For about 2 minutes Daniel wanders with no new note [P5.1.1]. A thought appears in the chat:
   > *You wonder: what does Lee actually want from Kuala Lumpur now?* [P5.2, P5.6]
6. **A minor character.** The British High Commissioner mentions, in passing, that London fears the federation
   splitting more than either party does. That's a discovery, so a note appears [G8.1].
7. **A story event: summons.** *"Mr Lee wants to see you at City Hall."* [P12, P14]
8. **Passing a note on.** At City Hall, Daniel uses the insert tool to tell Lee the Tunku's grievance about the
   mainland seats [P8, P8.1]. That matches a written trigger: Lee shifts from *defiant* to *calculating*, and a
   guarded discovery becomes revealable [P6, G4.1]. A subtle notice: *Lee goes quiet for a moment.* [P6.4]
   Lee then asks for something: *"Take a message to the Tunku. Tell him I'm willing to talk, but not to grovel."*
   [P11.2]
9. **The defining moment.** Daniel goes to the Tunku when he's ready [P9.5]. He has all the core discoveries, so
   there's no nudge [P9.6]. He chooses from four written acts [P9.1, P9.13]:
   - Pass on Lee's message frankly.
   - Soften it into an apology.
   - Withhold it and warn the Tunku that Lee is hardening.
   - *(deception)* Exaggerate the PAP's plans for the next election.

   Daniel chooses **frankly**.
10. **Immediate reaction.** The Tunku sighs: *"At least he's honest about it."* [P9.7]
11. **Prediction.** *What will the Tunku do now?* Daniel picks *"Agree to talks, but keep his guard up."*
    [P3.2, P3.3]
12. **Outcome by rule.** Message passed on frankly, plus the Tunku now *wary* rather than *hostile*, gives:
    **talks resume** [P9.3]. Invisibly, **ending 3 (emergency rule) is now closed** [P9.9, G6.3].

## Stage 2: After the riots (about 15 minutes)
**Stage opens** [G2.1, P3.4, P3.5, P9.8]:
> *Talks resumed in late 1964, but in July communal riots broke out in Singapore.*
> *You predicted the Tunku would agree to talks but stay guarded. He did, because Lee's message came frankly
> and the Tunku was wary rather than hostile.*

The situation has changed: the Tunku starts *wary* instead of *guarded*, and a new document is on the table.

**Central question:** *Can the federation survive the bitterness?*

1. **Story event: news.** *"Reports of fresh clashes in Geylang."* The Tunku's stance moves to *alarmed* [P14.1].
2. **A slant.** An UMNO official tells Daniel the riots were entirely the PAP's fault. Daniel finds a police report
   that contradicts this. There's no "contradicted" label [P13.4]; he notices the conflict himself and confronts
   the official with the report. That fires a trigger: the official backs off and reveals that the Tunku is under
   pressure from his own hardliners [P13.5, G10.4].
3. **A guarded discovery.** Because the Tunku is alarmed and Daniel already knows about the pressure, the Tunku
   opens up:
   > 📝 *The Tunku fears communal violence more than he fears Lee.* (core) [G5, G5.3]
4. **The secret ending stays hidden.** To unlock it, Daniel would need the Tunku at *confiding* and Lee's private
   confederation idea passed on to him [G6.1.2]. Daniel never finds Lee's idea, so the hidden act never appears
   [P9.11]. No hint ever points to it [P9.12].
5. **The final defining moment.** The Tunku asks Daniel's advice. Ending 3 is closed, so three acts are offered,
   one of which leads towards the historical outcome, unlabelled [P9.2, P9.10]:
   - Urge a cooling-off arrangement.
   - Tell him Singapore can't be held without more bloodshed.
   - Say nothing either way.

   Daniel chooses **"Tell him Singapore can't be held without more bloodshed."**
6. **Reaction, prediction, outcome.** The Tunku closes his eyes [P9.7]. Daniel predicts *"He'll ask Singapore to
   leave."* [P3.2] The rule gives the **historical ending** [P9.3].

## Epilogue (about 1 minute)

## The Interview (about 4 minutes)
> **A Straits Times journalist, 1966:** *"You advised the Tunku in those last weeks. Could separation have been
> prevented?"* [E2, G6.2]

- **Verdict:** Daniel picks *"No, it was coming either way."* [E2.1, G6.5]
- **Evidence:** he inserts 📝 *Utusan editorial* and 📝 *the PAP went back on its word*. [E2.2]
- **Push-back** (rule step 1, a note he holds that cuts the other way): *"Yet you wrote that the Tunku feared
  riots more than Lee. A cooling-off deal might have bought time. Doesn't that suggest room?"* [E2.4–E2.4.2]
- **Response:** Daniel changes his verdict to *"Only if both had compromised earlier."* [E2.5]
- **Close:** *"Thank you. That's a fair answer."* No judgement either way. [E2.6]
