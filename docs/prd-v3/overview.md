## What it is
A teacher's materials set the focus; the game draws on the wider historical record to fill in the rest. Students
step into a historical episode as a go-between. They question the people involved, weigh evidence that disagrees,
and influence real figures at moments where no option satisfies everyone. At the end, they defend their view in
an Interview, then see what actually happened. Nobody is graded.

## Outcomes
1. **Understand the episode** (primary): who was involved, what each wanted and feared, the key conflicts, what
   happened and why.
2. **Critical thinking** (developed along the way): question claims, cross-check, consider other perspectives,
   reason about consequences, weigh and reach a judgement.

## Pillars
1. Understanding first
2. Low floor, high ceiling
3. Characters are people, not encyclopaedias
4. The world pushes back
5. Hard choices, not a hard clock
6. The record stays apart from the simulation, without breaking immersion
7. Investigation takes real thought

## A session at a glance (default 45 minutes)
| Phase | What happens | Default time |
|---|---|---|
| Role briefing | Who you are, your stakes, your private brief | ~2 min |
| Stages (1–2, rarely 3) | Investigate, then act at a defining moment and predict the outcome | ~30 min total |
| Epilogue | How *your* run ended, labelled as your simulation | ~1 min |
| The Interview | Defend a verdict with your notes against one push-back | ~4 min |
| Reveal | What actually happened, where your run split from history, key corrections | ~6 min |

## Core mechanics
- **Discoveries become notes.** Characters reveal discoveries only when conditions are met; each one becomes a
  note in your own voice. Notes are the source of truth for what you know.
- **Stances and triggers.** Each major character has 3–5 stances (for example guarded → confiding), changed only
  by written triggers. Their portrait's expression shows it.
- **Passing notes on.** Insert a note into the chat to tell someone what you learned. Made-up claims are challenged
  and change nothing.
- **Documents and close reading.** Real excerpts with provenance that fits the period. Select the passage that
  matters to make a note. Some documents are slanted; no verdict labels during play.
- **Hints as your own thoughts.** Automatic when you're stuck, adapting to the time left, escalating from a
  question to a story event, never doing the work for you.
- **Story events.** 2–4 per stage: summons, characters moving, documents arriving, news, the final call.
- **Defining moments.** Each stage builds to a scene where a real figure decides based on what you advise, convey
  or keep back. Outcomes follow written rules (no dice).
- **Endings.** 1 historical, 2–3 alternatives, 0–1 secret. Earlier choices close some endings off. No good or bad
  labels.

## The teacher
Sets the timer, reshapes the generated adventure, reviews source labels, adds their own questions. Afterwards: a
navigable class summary (where students got stuck, predictions, verdicts, discoveries missed, endings reached,
anonymised answers, flagged messages).
