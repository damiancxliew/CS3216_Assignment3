# M4 — Target users and acquisition

## Who

| | Buyer / creator | Player |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Secondary-school history teachers (SG: Sec 3–4 and JC; equivalently KS3–4 / grades 8–11) | Their students, 14–18 |
| Need | A curriculum-specific, engaging lesson built from material they already teach, without game design | To understand a situation rather than memorise its outcome |
| Constraint | One free period to prepare; no budget authority; strong aversion to anything that states falsehoods confidently | Laptops in class, phones locked away; ~15 minutes of lesson time |

The teacher is the only user who has to be convinced. Students play what they are given,
so acquisition is entirely a teacher-side problem — and the share link means one
acquired teacher brings thirty users for free.

## Beachhead

Singapore secondary history departments teaching source-based case studies, plus the
IB/IGCSE history cohort, where source analysis is already the assessed skill. We are not
starting with "history teachers" in general: the wedge is teachers who already run
source-based work, because our grounded-claim-vs-assumption distinction is the thing
they are already grading.

## Channels, cheapest first

1. **Teacher-to-teacher inside a department.** The unit of adoption is a department
   meeting, not an individual. A published adventure is a link, so sharing a lesson that
   worked costs nothing — this is the primary loop and everything else feeds it.
2. **The share link itself.** Every student who plays sees a product they can show
   another teacher; the ending debrief is the most shareable artifact we have.
3. **Subject-teacher communities.** SG: MOE history subject chapters, the Singapore
   Council for the Teaching of History, school PLC sessions. Elsewhere: r/historyteachers,
   the #sschat and #historyteacher communities, Facebook groups for IB History.
4. **Conference and workshop demos.** A 20-minute hands-on session where a teacher
   brings their own PDF and leaves with a published adventure. The demo *is* the
   onboarding — our activation event is "published one adventure from their own source".
5. **Template library as SEO.** Published, teacher-corrected adventures for syllabus
   topics ("the fall of Singapore", "the Cuban missile crisis") are landing pages that
   rank for what teachers search the week before they teach it, and each is a one-click
   fork.
6. **School pilots.** One department per school, one term, free; in exchange we get usage
   data and a named reference. This is what converts to paid (M6).

## Activation and retention, as measured

Signup is not the metric; the funnel in M19 is instrumented so that **activation =
`adventure_published`** and **value delivered = `debrief_viewed` by a student**. The
retention question is whether a teacher publishes a *second* adventure in the same term,
because a one-off is a demo and a second one is a habit.

## What would falsify this plan

If teachers publish once and never return, the bottleneck is lesson-preparation trust,
not production cost, and the product needs a curated library before it needs self-serve
generation. The analytics are set up to detect that distinction rather than assume it.
